# Questions

## Four Background Questions

| # | Question | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which of these have you used to work with data? (Spreadsheets / SQL / Python or R / Dashboards / None of these) | Screening: at least one |
| 2 | How comfortable are you reading a table of records and an average computed from it? (1 to 5) | Screening: 3 or higher |
| 3 | Have you worked with New York City taxi data before? | Familiarity can explain fast noticing |
| 4 | How often do you have to act on a number someone else computed, without being able to check how? (Never / Sometimes / Often) | Added after M2 feedback, to show how close each participant is to the real situation of relying on other people’s numbers. |

## Briefing Shown on Screen

A labmate is writing a draft about taxi trips in New York City. They computed a few summary numbers from public taxi data and want to put them in the draft. They asked you to check each number before it goes in. Nobody computed these numbers by hand: the data is public. For each one, say whether the sentence is good enough to go into the draft.

Two ways to get this wrong: let a bad number through, or throw out rows that were actually fine.

## Decision Options for Each Case

“Your labmate wants to write:” followed by the sentence, then “Should this sentence go into the draft?”

| Option | Shown underneath |
|---|---|
| Yes, as it is | The number is fine to use. |
| Not yet, something needs checking | You can say what to check, but not what the answer is. |
| Yes, but with a warning | Use it, but say there is a concern. |
| Fix one thing first, then yes | You know what to fix. Once fixed, the number is fine. |

Then confidence from 1 to 7 and one line: “What did you look at to decide?”

## Debrief Read Aloud

Two of the four cases had a trip that I copied on purpose, so one trip was in the table twice. That is a real problem with a clear fix. The other two had trips with zero miles, which really happens in this data and has no clear fix, so saying “check this” is a fair answer. There was no right answer I was marking you against. I wanted to see what people look at. Any questions?
