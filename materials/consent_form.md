# Consent Form

This was the first screen. The first two agreements and the typed name were required; recording and anonymous quotes were optional. Participants typed their names only to record consent. Names are kept only in the original session files on my password-protected laptop and do not appear in the analysis sheets or the report.

Study. Explaining Trust in Aggregate Data Results · Sumaiya Azad · CS 6540, University of Utah

What this is. A study of how people decide whether to trust a number computed from data. You will see a few results from public New York City taxi records and say whether each one is good enough to use. It takes about 30 minutes.

What you will do. Four short questions about you, a warm-up that shows you the screen, four cases, and six questions at the end. During the cases, please think aloud: say what you are looking at and what you are thinking.

Not a test. There is no score and no right answer. Two people can look at the same screen and decide differently.

Recording and your data. With your permission, the session is audio-recorded. The recording stays on a password-protected computer and is deleted after the course is graded. Your name never appears anywhere; you are referred to as P1 through P6. Anonymous quotes may be used only if you agree to the terms below. The taxi data is public and has no passenger or driver names.

Voluntary. You can stop at any time, or ask for your data to be deleted later, without giving a reason. The first two background questions are needed to take part. You can skip any other question except the four cases: each needs a decision, a confidence rating, and one line before the screen moves on. If you would rather not answer a case, say so, and we stop.

Risks and questions. None beyond concentrating for a while. No payment. Ask the researcher anything at any time.
